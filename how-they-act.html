🌊 1. Tide Stage (Why Moving Water Wins)

Bass rarely burn energy hunting in dead slack water unless trapping bunker. On the Incoming Flood, high water lets big fish push over outer sandbars right into the shorebreak trough to pin bait against the dry sand. On the Outgoing Drop, Manasquan and Barnegat Inlets act like conveyor belts dumping crabs, mullet, and eels into the ocean—fish the jetty tips and outer sloughs.

🌕 2. Moon Phase (New vs. Full Moon)

Both New and Full moons create massive "Spring Tides" with extra current speed. New Moon nights are pitch black—bass rely heavily on their lateral line to feel vibration, making wide-wobbling darters, metal-lips, and black/blurple SP Minnows lethal. Full Moon nights often cause bass to feed late at night or during the afternoon change of tide—try bone or yellow needlefish under bright moonlight.

🧭 3. Barometric Pressure

Watch the barometer in the top bar of this site. When pressure is dropping below 29.90 inHg ahead of a front or Nor'easter, bass go on a feeding binge. When a cold front passes and pressure spikes above 30.25 inHg with bluebird skies, bass sink tight to the bottom and get lockjaw.

🔓 4. Our Personal Lockjaw Playbook

When you know fish are in front of you (rolling on bunker or bumping your plug without hooking up): 1) Tie a 3/0 white bucktail teaser 16" ahead of your plug, 2) Downsize leader to 30lb fluorocarbon, 3) Swap loud rattled plugs for a silent needlefish or unweighted soft plastic, and 4) Cut your retrieve speed in half.
