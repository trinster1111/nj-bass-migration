NJ Legal Harvest: 1 Fish at 28" to <31"

How to Bleed, Trim & Cook Slot Striped Bass

90% of how good a striped bass tastes is decided in the first 5 minutes on the beach and on the cutting board.

Step 1: On the Sand

Bleed Immediately

As soon as you measure a legal 28"–31" slot fish you plan to keep, cut the gill rakers with your pliers or knife and let it bleed out in the wash for 5 minutes before icing.

Step 2: On the Board

Trim the Dark Red Bloodline

After skinning, cut a shallow V-groove down the center of the fillet to remove all dark red lateral meat. That red strip holds 95% of any strong "fishy" oil.

Step 3: Zero Waste

Save Collars & Cheeks

Pop out the two round cheek medallions and saw off the pectoral collars. The collar meat is marbled like ribeye and is the best bite on the whole fish.

Recipe 01 • 10 Mins

Cast-Iron Blackened Slot Bass

Thick white loin chunks seared hard in butter and Cajun spice.

  1. Pat thick loin pieces completely dry with paper towels.
  2. Brush with melted butter and coat heavily in blackened seasoning (paprika, garlic, onion, thyme, cayenne, salt).
  3. Sear in a smoking-hot cast-iron skillet with 1 tbsp avocado oil + 1 tbsp butter for 3 mins per side. Squeeze fresh lemon on top.
Recipe 02 • 20 Mins

Crispy Salt-Crusted Bass Collars

The ultimate fisherman's appetizer off the grill or broiler.

  1. Scrape scales off the collar skin and pat bone-dry.
  2. Rub with olive oil, coarse kosher salt, cracked black pepper, and crushed garlic.
  3. Grill or broil skin-side up 6–7 minutes per side until skin blisters crispy and meat pulls easily from the fin bone.
Recipe 03 • 35 Mins

Jersey Shore Striper Chowder

Perfect for cold November nights after coming off the beach.

  1. Render diced thick-cut bacon in a Dutch oven; sauté onion, celery, garlic, and thyme.
  2. Add cubed Yukon gold potatoes, clam juice/fish stock, and simmer 12 mins until tender.
  3. Stir in heavy cream and 1.5 lbs of cubed striper fillets; poach gently for 5 minutes until flaky.
